    Research / Publication

    arXiv preprint · cs.LG, cs.AI · August 2026

    Clinical Graph-JEPA: Predictive Patient-State Knowledge Graphs for Cognitive Decision Support

    Kushagra Yadav, Nalin Prabhath, Amit Lamba, James E. Schrager, Goeun Han, Yining Mao

    Clinical records hold rich evidence about patient state, but extraction errors, ontology mismatch, missing relations, and temporal ambiguity all propagate downstream. The paper treats a clinical knowledge graph as a predictive patient-state representation rather than a static extraction artifact, combining multi-agent relation proposal, ontology-aware normalization, deterministic evidence scoring, and JEPA-based latent refinement over MIMIC-IV admissions.

    Evaluation Evaluated with leakage-free leave-one-out edge recovery and held-out batch-mask protocols. Injecting real discharge-note representations into note-grounded entities improved leave-one-out MRR by 31% relative, under the same cohort and evaluation protocol.

    My contribution I was part of the original hackathon team that built the system this paper grew out of, and presented it during the hackathon. On the paper itself I contributed to writing and editing and produced the poster and presentation materials, rather than the later methodology and implementation.

    In progress M.S. in Applied Data Science capstone · University of Chicago · Team project

    Multimodal HM-MRI and Pathology Learning for Lesion-Level Prostate Cancer Characterization

    Yining Mao, Lam Tran, Jacob Park

    This capstone explores how multimodal AI can combine prostate MRI and digital pathology to support prostate cancer characterization. MRI provides a broader view of suspicious regions within the prostate, while pathology offers detailed tissue-level information after biopsy or surgery. Our goal is to study how these two sources of information can be connected and learned together rather than analyzed in isolation.

    What we're building Working with clinical imaging data from the University of Chicago Medicine, our team is developing a pipeline for organizing, filtering, and preparing MRI and whole-slide pathology images for multimodal modeling. A major part of the project involves evaluating pathology slide quality, including issues such as tissue overlap, saturation, and vignetting, so that low-quality or uninformative images do not introduce noise into the training data.

    The research question From there, we are investigating ways to extract meaningful representations from each modality and align information across MRI and pathology. The broader research question is whether combining macroscopic imaging information with microscopic tissue features can produce a more complete representation of prostate cancer than either modality alone.

    The project has given me experience working with large-scale medical imaging data, multimodal machine learning, image quality assessment, and the practical challenges of building AI systems around real clinical data.
